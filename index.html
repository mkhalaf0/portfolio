01 — About

Low-power intelligent sensing
for human-centered environments

I am a Ph.D. student in Computer Engineering at Northeastern University, working within the Spatial Intelligence Research Group (SINRG) under Prof. Mallesham Dasari. My research sits at the intersection of wearable computing, wireless sensing, and edge intelligence, building systems that perceive and reason about physical space with minimal power budgets.

I investigate multimodal traffic dynamics in XR and AI glasses, develop capacitive sensing approaches for eye tracking, and design adaptive streaming and bitrate control for immersive content. My work spans Bluetooth, BLE, Wi-Fi Direct, and 5G protocols, always aiming to push the boundary of what lightweight, wearable devices can sense and understand.

Before starting my Ph.D., I earned my B.S. in Electrical & Computer Engineering from Norwich University (with minors in Mathematics and Computer Science), where I was named Student Engineer of the Year and served as President of Eta Kappa Nu.

02 — Research Areas

Sensing the invisible
architecture of space

Wireless Sensing & XR Traffic Analysis

Dissecting multimodal traffic dynamics across XR and AI glasses — measuring end-to-end latency, throughput, and power consumption over Bluetooth Classic, BLE, Wi-Fi Direct, and 5G. Benchmarking performance across Meta Ray-Ban, Dragon Glass, Even G1, and Brilliant Labs Frame under real-world conditions.

Bluetooth/BLE Wi-Fi Direct 5G Wireshark XR Traffic

Wearable Spatial Inference & Eye Tracking

Designing capacitive sensor-based eye tracking systems for power-efficient gaze estimation in mobile XR applications. Building AI-powered smart glasses for visually impaired users to increase mobility and independence through real-time spatial awareness and environmental understanding.

Capacitive Sensing Eye Tracking Smart Glasses Accessibility Low-Power

Adaptive Streaming & Edge Intelligence

Object-level dynamic bitrate control for live streaming XR scenes using geometric mesh representations. Conducting end-to-end network evaluation across real-world bandwidth traces to benchmark adaptive bitrate performance, demonstrating improved quality-of-experience and bandwidth efficiency.
